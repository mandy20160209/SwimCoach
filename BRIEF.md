# Project brief: SwimCoach

## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Elementary (grades K–5)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: December 18 (5:00 Eastern Time), and December 23 (the deadline of project).

## The problem
- People in swimming groups struggle with their speed because they don’t know the right posture.
- Impact area: Something else
- Why it matters: Their swimming skills will improve, and they can do better.
- Evidence it’s real: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26839604/
- What people told me: Person A: “My teacher said I always do the posture wrong, but I really don’t know why.”

## My solution
- My AI helps swimmers understand what they may be doing wrong and helps them fix it with a plan.
- Existing swimming apps can be useful, but SwimCoach will be free, more detailed, and let swimmers describe how they feel so the plan can fit them.

## Safety plan
- First, swimmers think about their own posture before the app gives an answer, so they keep learning instead of depending on the app for everything.
- The app must never encourage swimming alone, breath-holding, ignoring pain, or unsafe challenges.

## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. Scanning posture from the camera
2. Showing videos to fix posture
3. Learning about the user’s swimming knowledge
4. Giving correct, safe answers to real questions

## Maybe later (not yet)
- Show an AI response in detail about what the user does wrong
- Ask the user if the app can access the user’s camera

## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers, users can click a lesson to learn more or watch a posture video if they are unsure.
- Its job is to ask about the swimmer’s level and problems, make a plan, and suggest a posture video when needed.
- It talks to people who want to improve their swimming skills, including beginners, in simple and kind language.
- It must use safe swimming facts, camera information only when provided, and trusted posture video resources.
- It must not give unsafe advice, shame the swimmer, or make a plan above the user’s level.
- It should explain how better posture can improve swimming skills and speed.
